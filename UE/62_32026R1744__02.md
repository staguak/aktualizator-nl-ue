<p class="oj-normal">(39)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">To provide sufficient time for providers of high-risk AI systems and to clarify rules applicable to the AI systems already placed on the market or put into service before the relevant provisions of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 apply, it is appropriate to clarify the scope of the grace period provided in Article&nbsp;111(2) of that Regulation. For the purpose of that Article&nbsp;111(2), the grace period should apply where the type&nbsp;and model of AI system has already been placed on the market. This means that if at least one individual unit of the high-risk AI system has been lawfully placed on the market or put into service before the date specified in Article&nbsp;111(2), other individual units of the same type&nbsp;and model of high-risk AI system are subject to the grace period provided in that Article&nbsp;111(2) and thus may continue to be placed on the market, made available or put into service on the Union market without any additional obligations, requirements or the need for additional certification, as long as the design of that high-risk AI system remains unchanged. For the purposes of the application of the grace period provided in Article&nbsp;111(2), the decisive factor is the date on which the first unit of that type&nbsp;and model of high-risk AI system was placed on the market or put into service on the Union market for the first time. Any significant change to the design of that AI system after the date specified in Article&nbsp;111(2) should trigger the obligation of the provider to fully comply with all relevant provisions of that Regulation applicable to high-risk AI systems, including the conformity assessment requirements.</p>

<p class="oj-normal">Article&nbsp;113 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 establishes the dates of entry into force and application of that Regulation, in particular that the general date of application is&nbsp;2&nbsp;August 2026. For the obligations related to high-risk AI systems laid down in Sections&nbsp;1, 2 and 3 of Chapter&nbsp;III of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the delayed availability of standards, common specifications, and alternative guidance and the delayed establishment of national competent authorities lead to challenges that jeopardise the effective entry into application of those obligations and that risk a&nbsp;significant increase in implementation costs in a&nbsp;way that does not justify maintaining their initial date of application, namely 2&nbsp;August 2026. Therefore, it is appropriate that the date of application of Sections&nbsp;1, 2 and 3 of Chapter&nbsp;III is set to 2&nbsp;December 2027 for AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article&nbsp;6(2) and Annex&nbsp;III, and to 2&nbsp;August 2028 for AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article&nbsp;6(1) and Annex&nbsp;I. The distinction between the entry into application of the rules as regards AI systems classified as high-risk pursuant to Article&nbsp;6(2) and Annex&nbsp;III and Article&nbsp;6(1) and Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to that Regulation is consistent with the difference between the initial dates of application envisaged in Regulation (EU)&nbsp;2024/1689 and aims to provide the necessary time for adaptation and implementation of the corresponding obligations. The timely availability of support instruments, including guidance, relevant standards, common specifications and codes of practice is important in order to facilitate compliance and reduce the risk of divergent interpretation and uneven application of the rules across Member States. In order to ensure legal certainty and to avoid further delays in the application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Commission should ensure that measures in support of compliance with regard to Chapter&nbsp;III, Sections&nbsp;1, 2, and 3 of that Regulation are in place in due time to ensure timely and effective implementation of the necessary provisions.</p>

<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">In light of the objective to reduce implementation challenges for citizens, businesses and public administrations, it is essential that harmonised conditions for the implementation of certain rules are adopted only where strictly necessary. For that purpose, it is appropriate to remove certain empowerments bestowed on the Commission to adopt&nbsp;such harmonised conditions by means of implementing acts in cases where that is not the case. Article&nbsp;50(7), Article&nbsp;56(6), and Article&nbsp;72(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 should therefore be amended to remove the empowerments conferred on the Commission to adopt&nbsp;implementing acts. Given that the codes of practice referred to in Article&nbsp;50(7) and Article&nbsp;56(6) have limited legal effect, and in particular do not grant a&nbsp;presumption of conformity, it is not strictly necessary for these codes to be approved by an implementing act. Providers should be able to rely, pursuant to Article&nbsp;53(4) and Article&nbsp;54(2) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, on codes of practice assessed as adequate pursuant to Article&nbsp;56(6) thereof. The removal of the empowerment to adopt&nbsp;a&nbsp;harmonised template for a&nbsp;post-market monitoring plan in Article&nbsp;72(3) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 has the additional benefit of offering more flexibility for providers of high-risk AI systems to put in place a&nbsp;system for post-market monitoring that is tailored to their organisation. At the same time, recognising the need to offer clarity regarding how providers of high-risk AI systems are required to comply with their obligation set out in Article&nbsp;72(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, the Commission should be required to publish guidance, including a&nbsp;voluntary template, on the post-market monitoring plan by 2&nbsp;September 2027.</p>

<p class="oj-normal">(42)</p>

<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The use of artificial intelligence in machinery can help&nbsp;foster innovation and improve the efficiency of those machines. The application of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 of the European Parliament and of the Council&nbsp;<a id="ntc18-L_202601744EN.000101-E0018" href="62_32026R1744__04.md#ntr18-L_202601744EN.000101-E0018">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">18</span>)</a> and Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 might lead to overlaps. At the same time, it is important to ensure a&nbsp;level of protection with regard to risks related to the use of artificial intelligence in machinery that is consistent with the level of protection provided in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 with regard to high-risk AI systems. Given the specific nature of machinery and the machinery sector, and in order to address the need to simplify the regulatory framework for AI-enabled machinery, it is appropriate to move to a&nbsp;sectoral approach by moving Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 from Section&nbsp;A&nbsp;to Section&nbsp;B of Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. Accordingly, first, the application of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 to those machines should be limited to the provisions referred to in Article&nbsp;2(2) of that Regulation. The reference to Directive&nbsp;2006/42/EC should be moved from Section&nbsp;A&nbsp;to Section&nbsp;B of Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Regulation (EU)&nbsp;2024/1689 and updated so as to refer to Regulation (EU) 2023/1230. Second, it is crucial to ensure that Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 incorporates essential health and safety requirements for high-risk AI systems classified pursuant to Article&nbsp;6(1) of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and used as a&nbsp;safety component in machinery or high-risk AI systems constituting machinery that ensure a&nbsp;level of protection consistent with Regulation (EU)&nbsp;2024/1689. To that end, the Commission should be required to adopt&nbsp;delegated acts amending Annex&nbsp;III to Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 in order to reflect the relevant requirements set out in Chapter&nbsp;III, Section&nbsp;2, and Articles&nbsp;17, 19, 72 and 73 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. To avoid a&nbsp;legal gap&nbsp;and ensure alignment with the entry into application of the relevant high-risk AI system rules set out in Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, those delegated acts should apply by 2&nbsp;August 2028. For the same reasons, manufacturers should be free to rely on harmonised standards or common specifications referenced or adopted pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that cover the relevant essential requirements for the presumption of conformity within the meaning of Article&nbsp;20 of Regulation (EU) 2023/1230 until harmonised standards or common specifications regarding AI are referenced or adopted pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2023/1230.</p>

<p class="oj-normal">Conformity assessments of high-risk AI systems pursuant to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 can require the involvement of conformity assessment bodies. Only conformity assessment bodies that have been designated pursuant to that Regulation are able to carry out conformity assessments, and only for the activities related to the categories and types of AI systems concerned. To enable the specification of the scope&nbsp;of the designation of conformity assessment bodies notified pursuant to Article&nbsp;30 of Regulation (EU)&nbsp;2024/1689, it is necessary to draw up&nbsp;a&nbsp;list of codes, categories, and corresponding types of AI systems. The list of codes should take into account whether the AI system is a&nbsp;component of a&nbsp;product or itself a&nbsp;product covered by the Union harmonisation legislation listed in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (referred to as ‘AIP codes’, for AI systems covered by product legislation), or a&nbsp;system listed in Annex&nbsp;III to that Regulation, which currently concerns only biometric AI systems referred to in point (1) of Annex&nbsp;III (referred to as ‘AIB codes’, for biometric AI systems). Both AIP codes and AIB codes are vertical codes. The AIP codes are reference codes that provide a&nbsp;link to the Union harmonisation legislation listed in Section&nbsp;A&nbsp;of Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. The AIB codes are new codes specific to Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 that identify biometric AI systems referred to in point&nbsp;(1) of Annex&nbsp;III to that Regulation. The list of codes should also take into account specific types and underlying technologies of AI systems (referred to as ‘AIH codes’, for horizontal AI system codes). The AIH codes are new AI technology-specific codes and can be applied in conjunction with AIP or AIB vertical codes. The AIH codes cover the underlying types and technologies of AI systems. The list of codes, comprising three categories, should provide for a&nbsp;multi-dimensional typology of AI systems which ensures that conformity assessment bodies designated as notified bodies are fully competent in regard to the AI systems they are required to assess.</p>
