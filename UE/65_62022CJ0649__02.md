<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Accordingly, in so far as the objective of Directive 2008/104 is to ensure the protection of temporary agency workers as regards safety and health at work, it must be held, as the Commission has done, that if, in the event of an accident at work, the financial risk for user undertakings were lower as regards those workers compared with the workers they recruit directly, those undertakings would be less encouraged to invest in the safety of temporary agency workers, which would undermine that objective.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point58">58</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Consequently, the objectives pursued by Directive 2008/104 support the interpretation of the concept of ‘pay’, within the meaning of Article&nbsp;3(1)(f)(ii) of that directive, as a ‘basic working and employment condition’, within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of that directive, according to which that concept includes compensation to which a temporary agency worker is entitled in respect of the total permanent incapacity to carry out his or her usual occupation as a result of an accident at work sustained at the user undertaking.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point59">59</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Contrary to what the Spanish Government essentially claims, that interpretation is not affected by the fact that such compensation is paid after the termination of the temporary employment relationship or by the fact that that compensation allegedly arises solely from the declaration of total permanent incapacity of the temporary agency worker concerned to work and, therefore, from the termination of his employment relationship.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point60">60</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">First, it must be stated that the fact that the compensation at issue is paid after the termination of the employment relationship does not prevent them from being in the nature of pay within the meaning of Article&nbsp;3(1)(f)(ii) of Directive 2008/104 (see, by analogy, judgment of 8&nbsp;May 2019, Praxair MRC, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2019%3A379" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑486/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2019%3A379" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2019:379</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2019%3A379#point70" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">70</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point61">61</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Secondly, an interpretation of the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ which excludes the compensation that an employer must pay to a temporary agency worker solely because that compensation is linked to the termination of his or her employment relationship, from the scope of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, would be contrary both to the context of that provision and to the aims pursued by that directive (see, to that effect, judgment of 12&nbsp;May 2022, Luso Temp, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑426/20</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:373</a>, paragraphs&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point39" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">39</a> and&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point45" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">45</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point62">62</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Furthermore, as is apparent from the documents before the Court, the accident at work in question in the main proceedings, which is the event giving rise to&nbsp;XXX’s total permanent incapacity to carry out his or her usual occupation, occurred ‘[during his] assignment at [the] user undertaking’, within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, with the result that it cannot be accepted that the compensation paid on account of that incapacity arises solely from the termination of XXX’s employment relationship.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<p class="coj-count" id="point63">63</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the light of all of the foregoing, it must be held that the compensation payable to temporary agency workers in respect of a total permanent incapacity to carry out their usual occupation as a result of an accident at work sustained at the user undertaking and resulting in the termination of their temporary employment relationship falls within the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

The scope of the principle of equal treatment referred to in the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point64">64</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">As regards, in the second place, the scope of the principle of equal treatment, referred to in the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, it should be noted that, in accordance with that provision, the basic working and employment conditions of temporary agency workers must be, for the duration of their assignment at a user undertaking, at least those that would apply if they had been recruited directly by that undertaking to occupy the same job.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point65">65</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">It is therefore for the referring court to determine, first, the basic working and employment conditions which would apply to a temporary agency worker if he or she had been recruited directly by the user undertaking to occupy the same job as that which he or she actually occupies there for the same period of time and, more specifically, in the present case, the compensation to which the temporary agency worker in question would be entitled in respect of his total permanent incapacity to carry out his usual occupation as a result of an accident at work and resulting in the termination of his employment relationship. That court must, secondly, compare those basic working and employment conditions with those which are actually applicable to that temporary agency worker during the period of his or her assignment at that user undertaking, in order to ascertain, on the basis of all of the relevant circumstances at issue in the main proceedings, whether or not the principle of equal treatment has been complied with in the case of that temporary agency worker (see, to that effect, judgment of 12&nbsp;May 2022, Luso Temp, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑426/20</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:373</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point50" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">50</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point66">66</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the present case, the referring court states that, in the light of the interpretation by the Tribunal Supremo (Supreme Court) of Article&nbsp;11 of Law 14/1994, temporary agency workers are entitled, in the event of total permanent incapacity to carry out their usual occupation, only to compensation under Article&nbsp;42 of the collective temporary agency work agreement, which is less than the compensation to which workers directly recruited by the user undertaking are entitled under Article&nbsp;31 of the collective transport sector agreement. More specifically, according to the documents before the Court, XXX, as a temporary agency worker, is entitled to compensation of EUR&nbsp;10,500 on the basis of the first of those collective agreements, whereas, if he had been recruited directly by Serveo Servicios, he would be entitled to compensation of EUR&nbsp;60,101.21 in respect of the second of those collective agreements.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point67">67</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">If that is indeed the case, which it is for the referring court to ascertain, it would have to be held that, contrary to what is provided for in the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, during the period of his assignment at Serveo Servicios, XXX did not enjoy basic working and employment conditions which are at least equal to those which would have been applicable to him if he had been recruited directly by that user undertaking to occupy the same job for the same period.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point68">68</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In that regard, although the Member States may, under Article&nbsp;5(2) to&nbsp;(4) of Directive 2008/104, provide for the possibility, under certain specific conditions, of derogating from the principle of equal treatment, the order for reference and the documents before the Court contain no information regarding the implementation of any such derogation in Spain.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point69">69</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In addition, while it is true that, under Article&nbsp;5(3) of Directive 2008/104, the social partners are able to conclude collective agreements which establish arrangements concerning the working and employment conditions of temporary agency workers which may differ from those referred to in Article&nbsp;5(1), such agreements must, however, in accordance with Article&nbsp;5(3), read in conjunction with recitals&nbsp;16 and&nbsp;17 of that directive, guarantee the overall protection of temporary agency workers.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point70">70</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">The obligation to guarantee the overall protection of temporary agency workers requires, in particular, that they be granted advantages in terms of basic working and employment conditions which are such as to compensate for the difference in treatment suffered by those workers, and compliance with that obligation must be specifically assessed (see, to that effect, judgment of 15&nbsp;December 2022, TimePartner Personalmanagement, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A983" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑311/21</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A983" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:983</a>, paragraphs&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A983#point44" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">44</a> and&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A983#point50" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">50</a>). Accordingly, in order for the collective temporary work agreement to be able to derogate from the principle of equal treatment laid down in the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, that collective agreement must be able to provide XXX with such general protection by granting him countervailing benefits in respect of basic working and employment conditions which are capable of counterbalancing the effects of the difference in treatment which he has suffered, and it is for the referring court to determine whether that is the case.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point71">71</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Finally, the Court has consistently held that a national court, when hearing a case between individuals, is required, when applying the provisions of domestic law adopted for the purpose of transposing obligations laid down by a directive, to consider the whole body of rules of national law and to interpret them, so far as possible, in the light of the wording and purpose of that directive in order to achieve an outcome consistent with the objective pursued by the directive. However, an interpretation of national law that is <span class="coj-italic">contra legem</span> is precluded (see, to that effect, judgment of 12&nbsp;May 2022, Luso Temp, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑426/20</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:373</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point56" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">56</a> and&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point57" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">57</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point72">72</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Accordingly, if the referring court were to conclude that XXX would have been entitled to the compensation which he claims under Article&nbsp;31 of the collective agreement for the transport sector if he had been recruited directly by Serveo Servicios, it would be for that court, in particular, to determine whether Article&nbsp;11 of Law 14/1994 is capable of being interpreted in a manner consistent with the requirements of Directive 2008/104 and, therefore, to be interpreted in a manner other than by depriving XXX of that compensation, an interpretation which would be contrary to the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of that directive, as is apparent from paragraph&nbsp;67 above.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point73">73</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">It follows from all of the foregoing considerations that the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof, must be interpreted as precluding national legislation, as interpreted by national case-law, under which the compensation to which temporary agency workers are entitled in respect of a total permanent incapacity to carry out their usual occupation as a result of an accident at work sustained at the user undertaking and resulting in the termination of their temporary employment relationship, is less than the compensation to which those workers would be entitled, in the same situation and on the same basis, if they had been recruited directly by that user undertaking to occupy the same job for the same period of time.</p>
</td>
</tr>
