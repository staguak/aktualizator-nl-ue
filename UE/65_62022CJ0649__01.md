<p class="coj-normal">Article&nbsp;11(1) of Ley 14/1994 por la que se regulan las empresas de trabajo temporal (Law 14/1994 on temporary-work agencies), of 1&nbsp;June 1994 (BOE No&nbsp;131, of 2&nbsp;June 1994), in the version applicable to the dispute in the main proceedings (‘Law 14/1994’), provides:</p>
<p class="coj-normal">‘While working for a user undertaking, temporary agency workers shall be entitled to the basic working and employment conditions that would apply if they had been recruited directly by the user undertaking to occupy the same job.</p>
<p class="coj-normal">For these purposes, basic working and employment conditions means working and employment conditions relating to pay, the duration of working time, overtime, rest periods, night work, holidays and public holidays.</p>
<p class="coj-normal">Pay shall include all fixed or variable payments established in the collective agreement applicable to the user undertaking for the job to be performed and which relate to the job in question. It must include, in any event, the proportional part corresponding to weekly rest, annual bonuses and gratuities, public holidays and annual leave. The user undertaking is obliged to calculate the final amount to be paid to the worker and, to that end, to mention the payments referred to in this paragraph in the contract for the supply of the worker.</p>
<p class="coj-normal">Likewise, temporary agency workers shall be entitled to the same conditions that apply to workers in the user undertaking as regards&nbsp;… equal treatment for men and women, and any action to combat any discrimination based on sex, race or ethnic origin, religion, beliefs, disabilities, age or sexual orientation.’</p>

<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">As a preliminary point, it must be recalled that the need to arrive at an interpretation of EU law which will be of use to the referring court requires that court to define the factual and legislative context of the questions it is asking, or at the very least to explain the factual circumstances on which those questions are based. In the procedure established by Article&nbsp;267 TFEU, the Court is empowered to give rulings on the interpretation of EU legislation only on the basis of the facts which the national court puts before it (judgment of 12&nbsp;January 2023, DOBELES HES, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2023%3A1" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑702/20 and&nbsp;C‑17/21</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2023%3A1" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2023:1</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2023%3A1#point85" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">85</a> and the case-law cited).</p>

Consideration of the question referred

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">

The concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point37">37</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the first place, it is necessary to examine whether the compensation payable to temporary agency workers in respect of a total permanent incapacity to carry out their usual occupation as a result of an accident at work sustained at the user undertaking and resulting in the termination of their temporary employment relationship falls within the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point38">38</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Although the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104 does not indicate whether the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ referred to therein must be interpreted as including such compensation or not, that concept refers, inter alia, in accordance with the definition given in Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) of that directive, to the concept of ‘pay’.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point39">39</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In that regard, it should be noted that that concept is not defined by Directive 2008/104.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point40">40</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">While it is true that, in accordance with Article&nbsp;3(2) of Directive 2008/104, that directive ‘shall be without prejudice to national law as regards the definition of pay’, that provision cannot be interpreted as a waiver on the part of the EU legislature of its power itself to determine the scope of the concept of ‘pay’ within the meaning of that directive, since that provision merely means that that legislature intended to preserve the power of the Member States to define that concept for the purposes of national law, an aspect which that directive is not intended to harmonise (see, to that effect, judgment of 17&nbsp;November 2016, Betriebsrat der Ruhrlandklinik, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A883" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑216/15</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A883" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2016:883</a>, paragraphs&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A883#point30" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">30</a> to&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A883#point32" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">32</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point41">41</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">According to settled case-law, the meaning and scope of terms for which EU law provides no definition must be determined by reference to their usual meaning in everyday language, while account is also taken of the context in which they occur and the purposes of the rules of which they form part (judgment of 1&nbsp;August 2022, Navitours, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A608" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑294/21</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A608" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:608</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A608#point25" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">25</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point42">42</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">First, according to its usual meaning in everyday language, the concept of ‘pay’ is generally understood as the money paid in respect of certain work or the performance of a service.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point43">43</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In that regard, it should be recalled that, according to settled case-law, the essential characteristic of ‘pay’ lies in the fact that it constitutes consideration for the service in question and is normally agreed upon between the provider and the recipient of the service (judgment of 11&nbsp;November 2021, Manpower Lit, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2021%3A906" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑948/19</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2021%3A906" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2021:906</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2021%3A906#point45" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">45</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point44">44</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Furthermore, the concept of ‘pay’ is defined in Article&nbsp;157(2) TFEU as ‘the ordinary basic or minimum wage or salary and any other consideration, whether in cash or in kind, which the worker receives directly or indirectly, in respect of his employment, from his employer’. As is apparent from the case-law, that concept must be interpreted broadly and it covers, in particular, any consideration, whether in cash or in kind, whether immediate or future, provided that the worker receives it, albeit indirectly, in respect of his employment from his employer, and irrespective of whether it is received under a contract of employment, by virtue of legislative provisions or on a voluntary basis (judgment of 8&nbsp;May 2019, Praxair MRC, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2019%3A379" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑486/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2019%3A379" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2019:379</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2019%3A379#point70" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">70</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point45">45</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In addition, the Court has stated that consideration classified as ‘pay’ within the meaning of Article&nbsp;157 TFEU specifically includes consideration paid by the employer under a contract of employment whose purpose is to ensure that workers receive income even where, in certain specific cases, they are not performing any work provided for in their contracts of employment. Moreover, the fact that such benefits are in the nature of pay cannot be called in question merely because they can also be regarded as reflecting considerations of social policy (judgment of 19&nbsp;September 2018, Bedi, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2018%3A734" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑312/17</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2018%3A734" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2018:734</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2018%3A734#point34" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">34</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<p class="coj-count" id="point46">46</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In that regard, it should be noted that it is apparent from the case-law that ‘pay’ within the meaning of Article&nbsp;157(2) TFEU is one of the ‘employment conditions’ within the meaning of point&nbsp;1 of Clause 4 of the Framework Agreement on part-time work concluded on 6&nbsp;June 1997, which is annexed to Council Directive 97/81/EC of 15&nbsp;December 1997 concerning the Framework Agreement on part-time work concluded by UNICE, CEEP and the ETUC (<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/oj/JOL_1998_014_R_TOC" hreflang="en" onclick="target='CourtTab';">OJ 1998 L&nbsp;14, p.&nbsp;9</a>), as amended by Council Directive 98/23/EC of 7&nbsp;April 1998 (<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/oj/JOL_1998_131_R_TOC" hreflang="en" onclick="target='CourtTab';">OJ 1998 L&nbsp;131, p.&nbsp;10</a>) and point&nbsp;1 of Clause 4 of the framework agreement on fixed-term work concluded on 18&nbsp;March 1999, which is annexed to Council 2008, Impact, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2008%3A223" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" <td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">The first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104 is intended to provide effective protection for atypical and precarious workers in an even more targeted manner than those clauses, so an interpretation similar to that adopted in the case-law cited in paragraphs&nbsp;44 and&nbsp;45 above concerning the concept of ‘pay’ within the meaning of Article&nbsp;157 TFEU is necessary, a fortiori, in order to determine the scope of that concept, within the meaning of Article&nbsp;3(1)(f)(ii) of that directive (see, to that effect, judgment of 12&nbsp;May 2022, Luso Temp, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑426/20</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:373</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point36" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">36</a>).</p>
</td>

<p class="coj-count" id="point48">48</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">While it is true that compensation such as that referred to in paragraph&nbsp;37 above is not paid directly in return for work carried out by a temporary agency worker, such compensation nevertheless constitutes consideration in cash which (i) is received indirectly by the worker from his or her employer in respect of his or her employment, in so far as such compensation is provided for by a collective agreement applicable to the employment relationship, and&nbsp;(ii) is paid to the worker to compensate for the loss of income resulting from his or her incapacity to carry out his or her usual occupation, so that its purpose is to provide him or her with a source of income.</p>
</td>
</tr>

<p class="coj-count" id="point49">49</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Therefore, the concept of ‘pay’, within the meaning of Article&nbsp;3(1)(f)(ii) of Directive 2008/104, is sufficiently broad to cover compensation to which temporary agency workers are entitled in respect of a total permanent incapacity to carry out their usual occupation as a result of an accident at work sustained at the user undertaking.</p>
</td>
</tr>

<p class="coj-count" id="point50">50</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Secondly, as regards the context of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f)(ii) thereof, it should be noted that, according to recital&nbsp;13 of that directive, Directive 91/383 lays down the safety and health provisions applicable to temporary agency workers.</p>
</td>
</tr>

<p class="coj-count" id="point51">51</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">It is apparent from Article&nbsp;2(1) of Directive 91/383, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;1(2) thereof, that the purpose of that directive is to ensure that temporary agency workers are afforded, as regards safety and health at work, the same level of protection as that of other workers in the user undertaking. Furthermore, under Article&nbsp;2(2), read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;1(2), the existence of a temporary employment relationship cannot justify different treatment with respect to working conditions inasmuch as the protection of safety and health at work are involved, especially since, as is apparent from the fourth recital of that directive, temporary agency workers are, in certain sectors, more exposed to the risk of accidents at work than other workers.</p>
</td>
</tr>

<p class="coj-count" id="point52">52</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In addition, Article&nbsp;8 of Directive 91/383 provides, in essence, that Member States are to take the necessary steps to ensure that, without prejudice to the responsibility of the temporary-work agencies as laid down in national legislation, the user undertaking is responsible, for the duration of the assignment, for the conditions governing the performance of the work connected with safety, hygiene and health at work.</p>

<p class="coj-count" id="point53">53</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Accordingly, it must be held, first, that the protection of ‘safety’ and ‘health’ at work is covered by ‘working conditions’, within the meaning of Directive 91/383, and that the temporary agency worker must, in that regard, be treated, for the duration of the assignment, in the same way as workers employed directly by the user undertaking.</p>
</td>
</tr>

<p class="coj-count" id="point54">54</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Secondly, compensation such as that referred to in paragraph&nbsp;37 above is linked to the protection of ‘safety’ and ‘health’ at work in so far as the responsibility of the user undertaking and, as the case may be, of the temporary work agency, with regard to the conditions of performance of work associated with that protection, goes hand in hand with compensation for damage in the event that that protection fails, namely, in particular, where an accident at work occurs during the period of assignment of a temporary agency worker resulting in his or her total permanent incapacity to carry out his or her usual occupation.</p>
</td>
</tr>

</td>

<p class="coj-normal">Having regard to the reference made by Directive 2008/104 to Directive 91/383, it must therefore be held that the context of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof, supports the interpretation that the concept of ‘pay’, to which the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ in those provisions refers, includes compensation such as that referred to in paragraph&nbsp;37 above.</p>

<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Thirdly, as regards the objectives pursued by Directive 2008/104, it is apparent from recital&nbsp;1 of that directive that it seeks to ensure full compliance with Article&nbsp;31 of the Charter, paragraph&nbsp;1 of which establishes in general terms the right of every worker to working conditions that respect his or her health, safety and dignity. The Explanations relating to the Charter of Fundamental Rights (<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/oj/JOC_2007_303_R_TOC" hreflang="en" onclick="target='CourtTab';">OJ 2007 C&nbsp;303, p.&nbsp;17</a>) indicate, in that regard, that the concept of ‘working conditions’ must be understood in accordance with Article&nbsp;156 TFEU, although that provision does not define that concept. In the light of the objective of that directive to protect the rights of temporary agency workers, that lack of precision supports a broad interpretation of that concept (see, to that effect, judgment of 12&nbsp;May 2022, Luso Temp, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑426/20</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2022:373</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2022%3A373#point40" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">40</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
