<p class="coj-normal">Luso Temp contends, by contrast, that the calculation method to be used to determine the number of days of paid leave and the amount of the corresponding holiday bonus pay to which the applicants in the main proceedings are entitled is that provided for in the special rules on paid leave applicable to temporary agency workers, laid down in Article&nbsp;185(6) of the Labour Code, according to which the entitlement of temporary agency workers to paid leave and to the corresponding holiday bonus pay must be calculated in proportion to the duration of their contract. Accordingly, each of the applicants in the main proceedings is entitled to only 44&nbsp;days of paid leave corresponding to the two years worked.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point21">21</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">The referring court states that Article&nbsp;185 of that code is a specific rule applicable to temporary employment contracts, so it takes precedence over the general rules laid down by that code for most employment contracts. Given that it has been systematically incorporated into that code, it is clear that the intention of the legislature was to set aside the application of the general rules on leave.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point22">22</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">That court is uncertain as to the compatibility of Article&nbsp;185(6) of the Labour Code with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) and Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point23">23</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">According to that court, Article&nbsp;185(6) of that code introduces a difference in treatment between, on the one hand, temporary agency workers assigned to a user undertaking for a period greater than or equal to&nbsp;12&nbsp;months or for a period beginning during one calendar year and not ending until two calendar years or more after that date, and, on the other, workers who have been recruited directly by that user undertaking since the entitlement of temporary agency workers to paid leave and to the corresponding holiday bonus pay is always calculated in proportion to the duration of their contract while workers directly recruited by that user undertaking and occupying the same job there could, under the same circumstances, benefit from the more favourable general rules provided for in Articles&nbsp;237 to&nbsp;239 and&nbsp;245(1) of that code.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point24">24</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the present case, the consequence of that is that the applicants in the main proceedings are entitled to fewer days of paid leave and a lesser amount of the corresponding holiday bonus pay than they would be entitled to had they been recruited directly by the user undertaking at issue in the main proceedings for the same period of time and for the same job.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point25">25</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">The referring court notes that there is no such difference in treatment, however, where the duration of the temporary employment relationship is less than 12&nbsp;months or where it begins during one calendar year and ends during the following calendar year. In such situations, the number of days of paid leave and the amount of the corresponding holiday bonus pay for workers falling within the scope of the general rules is also calculated in proportion to the duration of their employment contract, pursuant to Article&nbsp;245(3) of the Labour Code, so that, in practice, there are no differences in treatment in such cases.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point26">26</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In those circumstances, the Tribunal Judicial da Comarca de Braga, Juízo do Trabalho de Barcelos (Braga District Court, Barcelos Labour Court) decided to stay the proceedings and to refer the following question to the Court of Justice for a preliminary ruling:</p>
<p class="coj-normal">‘Do Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) and Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive [2008/104] preclude a provision of law such as that in Article&nbsp;185(6) of the [Labour Code], under which temporary agency workers are, in all cases, entitled to paid [leave] and the corresponding holiday bonus pay only [in proportion] to the period of service in the user undertaking, even where their employment relationship commences in one calendar year and ends [two calendar years or more] later, whereas a worker recruited directly by the user undertaking who occupies the same job for the same period of time will be subject to the general [rules on leave] meaning that he or she will be entitled to a longer period of paid [leave] and more holiday bonus pay since these are not [in proportion] to the period of service?’</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

Consideration of the question referred

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point27">27</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">By its question, the referring court asks, in essence, whether the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof, must be interpreted as precluding national legislation under which the compensation to which temporary agency workers are entitled, in the event of the termination of their employment relationship with a user undertaking, in respect of days of paid annual leave not taken and the corresponding holiday bonus pay, is lower than the compensation to which those workers would be entitled, in the same situation and on the same basis, if they had been recruited directly by that user undertaking to occupy the same job for the same period of time.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

The concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’, within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point28">28</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the first place, it is necessary to examine whether compensation which is payable in the event of the termination of a temporary employment relationship, in respect of days of paid annual leave not taken and the corresponding holiday bonus pay, is covered by the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’, within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point29">29</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">According to settled case-law, in interpreting a provision of EU law, it is necessary to consider not only its wording but also the context in which it occurs and the objectives pursued by the rules of which it is part (judgment of 28&nbsp;October 2021, Magistrat der Stadt Wien (Grand Hamster&nbsp;– II), <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2021%3A881" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑357/20</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2021%3A881" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2021:881</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2021%3A881#point20" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">20</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point30">30</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">First, although the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104 does not indicate whether the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ referred to therein must be interpreted as including such compensation or not, that concept refers, in accordance with the definition given in Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) of Directive 2008/104, to both leave and pay.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point31">31</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Since that definition expressly refers to leave and since it is clear from recital&nbsp;1 of Directive 2008/104 that that directive is designed to ensure full compliance with Article&nbsp;31 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, which provides, inter alia, that every worker is entitled to an annual period of paid leave, the right to paid annual leave forms part of the ‘basic working and employment conditions’ within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of that Directive, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point32">32</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Secondly, as regards the context of which that provision forms part, it must be borne in mind that Directive 2008/104 was adopted to supplement the regulatory framework established by Directive 97/81/EC, as amended by Directive 98/23 and by Directive 1999/70, on the basis of Article&nbsp;137(1) and&nbsp;(2) EC, which empowered the EU institutions to adopt, by means of directives, minimum requirements for gradual implementation relating, inter alia, to working conditions (judgment of 14&nbsp;October 2020, KG (Successive assignments in the context of temporary agency work), <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑681/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2020:823</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823#point39" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">39</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point33">33</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">The Court has held, as regards the framework agreement on fixed-term work, that the expression ‘employment conditions’ should be understood to mean the rights, entitlements and obligations that define a given employment relationship, including both the conditions under which a person takes up employment and those concerning the termination of that relationship (judgment of 20&nbsp;December 2017, Vega González, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2017%3A1014" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑158/16</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2017%3A1014" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2017:1014</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2017%3A1014#point34" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">34</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point34">34</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">The Court has also held that the concept of ‘employment conditions’, within the meaning of clause&nbsp;4(1) of that framework agreement, includes the compensation that the employer must pay to an employee on account of the termination of his or her fixed-term employment contract (judgment of 14&nbsp;September 2016, de Diego Porras, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A683" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑596/14</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A683" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2016:683</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A683#point32" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">32</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point35">35</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Moreover, it is clear from the Court’s case-law relating to the framework agreement on part-time work that, with regard to a worker who has not been able, for reasons beyond his or her control, to exercise his or her right to paid annual leave before termination of the employment relationship, the compensation in lieu to which he or she is entitled must be calculated so that the worker is put in a position comparable to that which he or she would have been in had he or she exercised that right during his or her employment relationship (judgment of 11&nbsp;November 2015, Greenfield, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2015%3A745" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑219/14</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2015%3A745" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2015:745</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2015%3A745#point51" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">51</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point36">36</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Furthermore, as the Advocate General has, in essence, stated in points&nbsp;59 and&nbsp;60 of his Opinion, the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104 is intended to provide effective protection for atypical and precarious workers in an even more targeted manner than clause&nbsp;4(1) of the framework agreement on part-time work and clause&nbsp;4(1) of the framework agreement on fixed-term work, so a solution similar to that adopted in the case-law cited in paragraphs&nbsp;33 to&nbsp;35 above concerning the interpretation of the concept of ‘employment conditions’ within the meaning of those clauses is necessary, a fortiori, in order to determine the scope of the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’, within the meaning of that Article&nbsp;5.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point37">37</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Under Article&nbsp;5, which establishes the principle of equal treatment, ‘the basic working and employment conditions of temporary agency workers shall be, for the duration of their assignment at a user undertaking, at least those that would apply if they had been recruited directly by that undertaking to occupy the same job’, whereas, in accordance with clause&nbsp;4 of each of the abovementioned framework agreements, both of which lay down the principle of non-discrimination, fixed-term and part-time workers ‘shall not be treated in a less favourable manner’ than, respectively, comparable permanent workers and comparable full-time workers.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point38">38</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Lastly, the Court has held that, on termination of the employment relationship and when therefore it is no longer possible to take paid annual leave, Article&nbsp;7(2) of Directive 2003/88/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council of 4&nbsp;November 2003 concerning certain aspects of the organisation of working time (<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/oj/JOL_2003_299_R_TOC" hreflang="en" onclick="target='CourtTab';">OJ 2003 L&nbsp;299, p.&nbsp;9</a>) provides that the worker is entitled to compensation in lieu in order to prevent this impossibility leading to a situation in which the worker loses all enjoyment of that right, even in pecuniary form (judgment of 20&nbsp;July 2016, Maschek, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A576" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑341/15</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A576" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2016:576</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A576#point26" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">26</a> and the case-law cited).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point39">39</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Therefore, the context of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104 supports the interpretation that the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ referred to in that provision must be interpreted as including compensation payable by the employer on account of the termination of a temporary employment relationship in respect of days of paid annual leave not taken and the corresponding holiday bonus pay.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point40">40</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">As regards, thirdly, the objectives pursued by Directive 2008/104, that directive, as indicated in paragraph&nbsp;31 above, is designed to ensure full compliance with Article&nbsp;31 of the Charter of Fundamental Rights, paragraph&nbsp;1 of which establishes in general terms the right of every worker to working conditions that respect his or her health, safety and dignity. The Explanations relating to the Charter of Fundamental Rights (<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/oj/JOC_2007_303_R_TOC" hreflang="en" onclick="target='CourtTab';">OJ 2007 C&nbsp;303, p.&nbsp;17</a>) indicate, in that regard, that the expression ‘working conditions’ must be understood in accordance with Article&nbsp;156 TFEU. However, that provision merely refers, without any further definition, to ‘working conditions’ as being one of the areas of the European Union’s social policy in which the European Commission may intervene to encourage cooperation between Member States and facilitate the coordination of their action. In the light of the objective of that directive to protect the rights of temporary agency workers, that lack of precision supports a broad interpretation of the concept of ‘working conditions’ (judgment of 14&nbsp;October 2020, KG (Successive assignments in the context of temporary agency work), <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑681/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2020:823</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823#point54" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">54</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point41">41</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Furthermore, it is clear from recitals&nbsp;10 and&nbsp;12 of Directive 2008/104 that, given that there are considerable differences in the use of temporary agency work and in the legal situation, status and working conditions of temporary agency workers within the European Union, that directive is intended to establish a protective framework for those workers which is non-discriminatory, transparent and proportionate, while respecting the diversity of labour markets and industrial relations. Accordingly, under Article&nbsp;2 of that directive, the purpose of that directive is to ensure the protection of temporary agency workers and to improve the quality of temporary agency work by ensuring that the principle of equal treatment is applied to those workers and by recognising temporary-work agencies as employers, while taking into account the need to establish a suitable framework for the use of that type of work with a view to contributing effectively to the creation of jobs and to the development of flexible forms of working (judgment of 14&nbsp;October 2020, KG (Successive assignments in the context of temporary agency work), <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑681/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2020:823</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823#point40" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">40</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point42">42</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Furthermore, it must be pointed out that recital&nbsp;11 of Directive 2008/104 states that that directive is intended to meet not only undertakings’ needs for flexibility, but also employees’ need to reconcile their working and private lives and thus contributes to job creation and to participation and integration in the labour market. That directive is therefore designed to reconcile the objective of flexibility sought by undertakings and the objective of security corresponding to the protection of workers (judgment of 14&nbsp;October 2020, KG (Successive assignments in the context of temporary agency work), <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑681/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2020:823</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823#point50" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">50</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point43">43</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">That twofold objective thus gives expression to the intention of the EU legislature to bring the conditions of temporary agency work closer to ‘normal’ employment relationships, especially since, in recital&nbsp;15 of Directive 2008/104, the EU legislature has expressly stated that employment contracts for an indefinite term are the general form of employment. That directive therefore also aims to stimulate temporary agency workers’ access to permanent employment at the user undertaking. The principle of equal treatment, as laid down in Article&nbsp;5(1) of that directive, contributes to that twofold objective (judgment of 14&nbsp;October 2020, KG (Successive assignments in the context of temporary agency work), <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑681/18</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2020:823</a>, paragraphs&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823#point51" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">51</a> and&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2020%3A823#point52" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">52</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point44">44</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">As the Advocate General noted in point&nbsp;39 of his Opinion, the balance between the promotion of employment and security on the labour market can only be achieved if that principle of equal treatment is fully respected.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point45">45</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Consequently, an interpretation of the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ which excludes the compensation that an employer must pay to a temporary agency worker on account of the termination of his or her employment relationship from the scope of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104 is contrary to the objectives pursued by that directive which are set out in paragraphs&nbsp;40 to&nbsp;44 above.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point46">46</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">First, such an interpretation would limit the scope of the protection granted to temporary agency workers as regards equal treatment in disregard of one of the objectives assigned to that provision (see, by analogy, judgment of 14&nbsp;September 2016, de Diego Porras, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A683" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xml;notice=branch" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">C‑596/14</a>, <a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A683" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/pdf" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">EU:C:2016:683</a>, paragraph&nbsp;<a href="http://publications.europa.eu/resource/ecli/ECLI%3AEU%3AC%3A2016%3A683#point30" onclick="target='CourtTab';" type="application/xhtml+xml" hreflang="en" class="coj-CourtLink">30</a>).</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point47">47</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">Secondly, that interpretation would have the consequence that the principle of equal treatment would cease to apply upon termination of the temporary agency worker’s contract, with the result that it would favour the termination of temporary contracts over the implementation of the objective pursued by Directive 2008/104, as recalled in paragraph&nbsp;43 above, of stimulating temporary agency workers’ access to permanent employment.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point48">48</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the light of the foregoing, the concept of ‘basic working and employment conditions’ within the meaning of the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, read in conjunction with Article&nbsp;3(1)(f) thereof, must be interpreted as including the compensation that an employer must pay to a worker on account of the termination of his or her temporary employment relationship in respect of days of paid annual leave not taken and the corresponding holiday bonus pay.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

The scope of the principle of equal treatment referred to in the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point49">49</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">In the second place, as regards the scope of the principle of equal treatment, in accordance with the first subparagraph of Article&nbsp;5(1) of Directive 2008/104, the basic working and employment conditions of temporary agency workers must be, for the duration of their assignment at a user undertaking, at least those that would apply if they had been recruited directly by that undertaking to occupy the same job.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="5%">
<col width="95%">
</colgroup><tbody><tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-count" id="point50">50</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">It is therefore for the referring court to determine, first, the basic working and employment conditions which would apply to the temporary worker if he or she had been recruited directly by the user undertaking to occupy the same job as that which he or she actually occupies there for the same period of time and, more specifically, in the present case, the compensation to which he or she would be entitled, on account of the termination of his or her temporary employment relationship, in respect of days of paid annual leave not taken and the corresponding holiday bonus pay. That court must, secondly, compare those basic working and employment conditions with those which are actually applicable to that temporary agency worker during the period of his or her assignment at that user undertaking, as the Advocate General stated, in essence, in point&nbsp;60 of his Opinion, in order to ascertain, on the basis of all the relevant circumstances at issue in the main proceedings, whether or not the principle of equal treatment has been complied with in the case of that temporary agency worker.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>

<p class="coj-count" id="point51">51</p>

<p class="coj-normal">In the present case, the referring court states, inter alia, that temporary agency workers who enter the service of a user undertaking in one calendar year and do not cease their activity until two calendar years or more after that entry into service are in a less favourable situation, for the duration of their assignment at that user undertaking, than they would have been if they had been recruited directly by the user undertaking to occupy the same job for the same period of time.</p>
</td>

<td valign="top">
<p class="coj-normal">According to the referring court, temporary agency workers are entitled, in accordance with Article&nbsp;185(6) of the Labour Code, only to leave and to holiday bonus pay calculated in proportion to their period of service while workers recruited directly by a user undertaking are entitled to paid leave under the general rules laid down in Articles&nbsp;237 to&nbsp;239 and&nbsp;245 of that code. That means that, in the present case, each of the applicants in the main proceedings is entitled to&nbsp;44&nbsp;days’ paid leave whereas, if they had been recruited directly by the user undertaking at issue in the main proceedings to occupy exactly the same job for the same period of time, GD would be entitled to&nbsp;67&nbsp;days’ paid leave and ES would be entitled to&nbsp;65&nbsp;days’ paid leave.</p>
