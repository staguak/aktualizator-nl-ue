            DIRECTIVE (EU) 2026/2450 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL

of 21&nbsp;October 2026

amending Directive 2004/37/EC on the protection of workers from the risks related to exposure to carcinogens, mutagens or reprotoxic substances at work

(Text with EEA relevance)

<a id="pbl_1"></a>

THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL OF THE EUROPEAN UNION,

<a id="cit_1"></a>

Having regard to the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, and in particular Article&nbsp;153(2), point&nbsp;(b), in conjunction with Article&nbsp;153(1), point&nbsp;(a), thereof,

<a id="cit_2"></a>

Having regard to the proposal from the European Commission,

<a id="cit_3"></a>

After transmission of the draft legislative act to the national parliaments,

<a id="cit_4"></a>

Having regard to the opinion of the European Economic and Social Committee&nbsp;<a id="ntc1-L_202602450EN.000101-E0001" href="#ntr1-L_202602450EN.000101-E0001">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">1</span>)</a>,

<a id="cit_5"></a>

After consulting the Committee of the Regions,

<a id="cit_6"></a>

Acting in accordance with the ordinary legislative procedure&nbsp;<a id="ntc2-L_202602450EN.000101-E0002" href="#ntr2-L_202602450EN.000101-E0002">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">2</span>)</a>,

Whereas:

                        <td valign="top">
                           <p class="oj-normal">(1)</p>
                        </td>
                        <td valign="top">
                           <p class="oj-normal">In order to improve the protection of workers against risks from exposure to carcinogens, mutagens or reprotoxic substances at the place of work and ensure the same minimum level of protection across the Union, regular updates of Directive 2004/37/EC of the European Parliament and the Council&nbsp;<a id="ntc3-L_202602450EN.000101-E0003" href="#ntr3-L_202602450EN.000101-E0003">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">3</span>)</a> are necessary. Occupational exposure limit (OEL) values should be established or revised in light of available information, including up-to-date scientific evidence and technical data, and should be based on a&nbsp;thorough assessment of the socio-economic impact and feasibility factors. That information should, where available, include opinions of the Committee for Risk Assessment (RAC) of the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) established by Regulation (EC)&nbsp;No&nbsp;1907/2006 of the European Parliament and of the Council&nbsp;<a id="ntc4-L_202602450EN.000101-E0004" href="#ntr4-L_202602450EN.000101-E0004">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">4</span>)</a> and opinions of the Advisory Committee on Safety and Health at Work (ACSH) established by Council Decision of 22&nbsp;July 2003&nbsp;<a id="ntc5-L_202602450EN.000101-E0005" href="#ntr5-L_202602450EN.000101-E0005">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">5</span>)</a>. Those opinions provide necessary scientific evidence to substantiate any Commission proposal to amend Directive 2004/37/EC. Moreover, they are based on practical experience and the realities of the place of work across the Union and reflect a&nbsp;broad consensus. ACSH opinions, which are the outcome of tripartite consensus, are of particular importance in that context, and should be taken into account. In accordance with the Interinstitutional Agreement of 13&nbsp;April 2016 on Better Law-Making&nbsp;<a id="ntc6-L_202602450EN.000101-E0006" href="#ntr6-L_202602450EN.000101-E0006">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">6</span>)</a>, the Commission should ensure that its proposals are informed by robust evidence and comprehensive stakeholder involvement, and should systematically assess the economic, social and environmental impact of its proposals. Its proposals should respect the principles of subsidiarity and proportionality, in accordance with the Treaty on European Union (TEU) and the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union (TFEU).</p>
                        </td>
                     </tr>
                  </tbody>
               </table>

                           <p class="oj-normal">(2)</p>
                        </td>
                        <td valign="top">
                           <p class="oj-normal">Directive 2004/37/EC covers substances or mixtures which meet the criteria for classification as a&nbsp;category 1A or 1B carcinogen, mutagen or reproductive toxicant set out in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Regulation (EC)&nbsp;No&nbsp;1272/2008 of the European Parliament and of the Council&nbsp;<a id="ntc7-L_202602450EN.000101-E0007" href="#ntr7-L_202602450EN.000101-E0007">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">7</span>)</a>, as well as substances, mixtures or processes referred to in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to that Directive. Robust scientific evidence is to be provided for any new addition to the list of substances, mixtures and processes referred to in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Directive 2004/37/EC to demonstrate that those substances, mixtures and processes fall within the scope&nbsp;of that Directive, based on available valid scientific sources, such as the ECHA, the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC) and national bodies, paying particular attention to peer-reviewed published literature on those substances, mixtures and processes. To ensure the highest level of worker protection, the Commission should enhance its efforts to identify priority substances for setting limit values under that Directive on the basis of the best available information, including scientific and technical data, while making best use of current and future budgetary and administrative resources, taking into account scientific and administrative needs.</p>

                        <td valign="top">
                           <p class="oj-normal">The substances, mixtures and processes referred to in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Directive 2004/37/EC, and the substances and mixtures released by those processes, vary as to whether they have carcinogenic, mutagenic or reprotoxic effects. It is therefore appropriate to clarify the definitions of ‘carcinogen’, ‘mutagen’ and ‘reprotoxic substance’ provided for in that Directive, to ensure that the relevant substances, mixtures and processes are covered by each of those definitions. It is also appropriate to align the definition of ‘reprotoxic substance’ with the definitions of ‘carcinogen’ and ‘mutagen’.</p>

                        </td>
                        <td valign="top">
                           <p class="oj-normal">For mutagens and most carcinogens, it is not scientifically possible to identify levels below which exposure would not lead to adverse health effects. Although setting limit values for exposure at the place of work in relation to carcinogens and mutagens in Directive&nbsp;2004/37/EC contributes to a&nbsp;significant reduction of risks arising from such exposure by means of the stepwise and goal-setting approach adopted in that Directive, it nonetheless does not completely eliminate risks to the health and safety of workers arising from exposure at work (residual risks). Therefore, it is essential that the existence of such residual risks be communicated to workers in a&nbsp;clear and transparent manner during the training of workers provided for in that Directive. A&nbsp;list of residual risks associated with the current binding OELs for carcinogens under Directive 2004/37/EC and Directive&nbsp;2009/148/EC of the European Parliament and of the Council&nbsp;<a id="ntc8-L_202602450EN.000101-E0008" href="#ntr8-L_202602450EN.000101-E0008">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">8</span>)</a> was adopted by consensus by the ACSH in its opinion entitled ‘Communication of the residual risks associated with the binding OELs of carcinogens’ of 10&nbsp;December 2025.</p>

While advancing the industrial transition, stimulating the circular economy and maintaining and enhancing the international strategic autonomy in raw materials are all priorities of the Union, it is essential to ensure that workers receive a&nbsp;high and comparable level of protection against health risks related to occupational exposure. Principle 10 of the European Pillar of Social Rights&nbsp;<a id="ntc9-L_202602450EN.000101-E0009" href="#ntr9-L_202602450EN.000101-E0009">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">9</span>)</a> also recognises the right of workers to a&nbsp;high level of protection of their health and safety at work, which includes protection from exposure to carcinogens, mutagens and reprotoxic substances at the place of work. In that regard, the process for setting OEL values takes into account not only scientific and health considerations, but also socioeconomic aspects, which in some cases justify the establishment of transitional periods.</p>

                           <p class="oj-normal">(7)</p>
                        </td>
                        <td valign="top">
                           <p class="oj-normal">The IARC classified welding fumes as ‘carcinogenic to humans’ (Group&nbsp;1 of the IARC classification). According to the ECHA Scoping Study report for evaluation of limit values for welding fumes and fumes from other processes that generate fume in a&nbsp;similar way at the workplace (the ‘ECHA Scoping Study report’), welding fumes and fumes from other processes that generate fumes in a&nbsp;similar way are complex and can include carcinogens, mutagens or reprotoxic substances, such as chromium(VI) compounds, nickel compounds, cadmium and its inorganic compounds. The ACSH, having considered the latest available scientific evidence, including the ECHA Scoping Study report as regards the impact on workers’ health and safety, recommended including in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Directive 2004/37/EC only work involving exposure to welding fumes. The complexity and heterogeneity of welding fumes, together with the absence of harmonised classification in Regulation (EC)&nbsp;No&nbsp;1272/2008, contribute to a&nbsp;lack of clarity regarding the extent of their possible danger to workers, and therefore a&nbsp;lack of appropriate risk management measures at the place of work. Addressing that absence of classification for welding fumes at Union level would ensure more legal clarity with regard to the application of Directive 2004/37/EC. It is therefore appropriate, in line with the opinion of the ACSH, to include in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Directive 2004/37/EC work involving exposure to fumes from welding processes, containing substances or mixtures which meet the criteria for classification as a&nbsp;category&nbsp;1A or 1B carcinogen, mutagen or reproductive toxicant set out in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Regulation (EC)&nbsp;No&nbsp;1272/2008. That ACSH opinion also included a&nbsp;recommendation to develop&nbsp;guidance on welding fumes. In addition to the existing guidance, such as the Guidance for National Labour Inspectors on addressing health risks from Welding Fume developed by the Senior Labour Inspectors’ Committee in 2018, further Union guidance, in line with the EU Strategic Framework on Health and Safety at work on the basis of the latest scientific evidence, could assist labour inspectors and enterprises, especially small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs), including microenterprises, in ensuring compliance with the welding fumes entry in Annex&nbsp;I&nbsp;to Directive 2004/37/EC. Such guidance could serve to promote, inter alia, a&nbsp;common minimum high level of protection across the Member States for all workers exposed to welding fumes.</p>

OELs for cobalt and inorganic cobalt compounds are thus necessary to help&nbsp;prevent long-term effects on the health and wellbeing of workers and to support the attractiveness, competitiveness and long-term sustainability of the cobalt industry in the Union, while avoiding the relocation of cobalt-processing enterprises to third countries with less stringent occupational safety and health regulations.</p>

<a id="d1e612-1-1"></a>Article&nbsp;2

<a id="002.001"></a>

1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Member States shall bring into force the laws, regulations and administrative provisions necessary to comply with this Directive by 20&nbsp;November 2028. They shall immediately inform the Commission thereof.

When Member States adopt&nbsp;those measures, they shall contain a&nbsp;reference to this Directive or be accompanied by such reference on the occasion of their official publication. The methods of making such reference shall be laid down by Member States.

<a id="002.002"></a>

2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Member States shall communicate to the Commission the text of the main measures of national law which they adopt&nbsp;in the field covered by this Directive.
