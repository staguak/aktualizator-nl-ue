            REGULATION (EC) NO 883/2004 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL

of 29 April 2004

on the coordination of social security systems

(Text with relevance for the EEA and for Switzerland)

<a id="pbl_1"></a>

THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND THE COUNCIL OF THE EUROPEAN UNION,

<a id="cit_1"></a>

Having regard to the Treaty establishing the European Community, and in particular Articles 42 and 308 thereof,

<a id="cit_2"></a>

Having regard to the proposal from the Commission presented after consultation with the social partners and the Administrative Commission on Social Security for Migrant Workers&nbsp;<a id="ntc1-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0001" href="51_32004R0883__04.md#ntr1-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0001">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">1</span>)</a>,

<a id="cit_3"></a>

Having regard to the Opinion of the European Economic and Social Committee&nbsp;<a id="ntc2-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0002" href="51_32004R0883__04.md#ntr2-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0002">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">2</span>)</a>,

<a id="cit_4"></a>

Acting in accordance with the procedure laid down in Article 251 of the Treaty&nbsp;<a id="ntc3-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0003" href="51_32004R0883__04.md#ntr3-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0003">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">3</span>)</a>,

Whereas:

<p class="oj-normal">(1)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The rules for coordination of national social security systems fall within the framework of free movement of persons and should contribute towards improving their standard of living and conditions of employment.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_2"></a>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="4%">

</colgroup><tbody>
<tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">(2)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The Treaty does not provide powers other than those of Article 308 to take appropriate measures within the field of social security for persons other than employed persons.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_3"></a>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">

<p class="oj-normal">(3)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">Council Regulation (EEC) No 1408/71 of 14 June 1971 on the application of social security schemes to employed persons, to self-employed persons and to members of their families moving within the Community&nbsp;<a id="ntc4-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0004" href="51_32004R0883__04.md#ntr4-L_2004166EN.01000101-E0004">(<span class="oj-super oj-note-tag">4</span>)</a> has been amended and updated on numerous occasions in order to take into account not only developments at Community level, including judgments of the Court of Justice, but also changes in legislation at national level. Such factors have played their part in making the Community coordination rules complex and lengthy. Replacing, while modernising and simplifying, these rules is therefore essential to achieve the aim of the free movement of persons.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p class="oj-normal">(4)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">It is necessary to respect the special characteristics of national social security legislation and to draw up only a system of coordination.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p class="oj-normal">(5)</p>

<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">It is necessary, within the framework of such coordination, to guarantee within the Community equality of treatment under the different national legislation for the persons concerned.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The close link between social security legislation and those contractual provisions which complement or replace such legislation and which have been the subject of a decision by the public authorities rendering them compulsory or extending their scope may call for similar protection with regard to the application of those provisions to that afforded by this Regulation. As a first step, the experience of Member States who have notified such schemes might be evaluated.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p class="oj-normal">(7)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">Due to the major differences existing between national legislation in terms of the persons covered, it is preferable to lay down the principle that this Regulation is to apply to nationals of a Member State, stateless persons and refugees resident in the territory of a Member State who are or have been subject to the social security legislation of one or more Member States, as well as to the members of their families and to their survivors.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>

<p class="oj-normal">(8)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The general principle of equal treatment is of particular importance for workers who do not reside in the Member State of their employment, including frontier workers.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_9"></a>

<p class="oj-normal">(9)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The Court of Justice has on several occasions given an opinion on the possibility of equal treatment of benefits, income and facts; this principle should be adopted explicitly and developed, while observing the substance and spirit of legal rulings.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_10"></a>

</colgroup><tbody>
<tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">(10)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">However, the principle of treating certain facts or events occurring in the territory of another Member State as if they had taken place in the territory of the Member State whose legislation is applicable should not interfere with the principle of aggregating periods of insurance, employment, self-employment or residence completed under the legislation of another Member State with those completed under the legislation of the competent Member State. Periods completed under the legislation of another Member State should therefore be taken into account solely by applying the principle of aggregation of periods.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_11"></a>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">
<colgroup><col width="4%">

</colgroup><tbody>
<tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">(11)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The assimilation of facts or events occurring in a Member State can in no way render another Member State competent or its legislation applicable.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_12"></a>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">

<p class="oj-normal">(12)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">In the light of proportionality, care should be taken to ensure that the principle of assimilation of facts or events does not lead to objectively unjustified results or to the overlapping of benefits of the same kind for the same period.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_13"></a>

<tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">(13)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">The coordination rules must guarantee that persons moving within the Community and their dependants and survivors retain the rights and the advantages acquired and in the course of being acquired.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_14"></a>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">

</colgroup><tbody>
<tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">(14)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">These objectives must be attained in particular by aggregating all the periods taken into account under the various national legislation for the purpose of acquiring and retaining the right to benefits and of calculating the amount of benefits, and by providing benefits for the various categories of persons covered by this Regulation.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_15"></a>

<table width="100%" border="0" cellspacing="0" cellpadding="0">

</colgroup><tbody>
<tr>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">(15)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">It is necessary to subject persons moving within the Community to the social security scheme of only one single Member State in order to avoid overlapping of the applicable provisions of national legislation and the complications which could result therefrom.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_16"></a>

</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">Within the Community there is in principle no justification for making social security rights dependent on the place of residence of the person concerned; nevertheless, in specific cases, in particular as regards special benefits linked to the economic and social context of the person involved, the place of residence could be taken into account.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<p class="oj-normal">(17)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">With a view to guaranteeing the equality of treatment of all persons occupied in the territory of a Member State as effectively as possible, it is appropriate to determine as the legislation applicable, as a general rule, that of the Member State in which the person concerned pursues his activity as an employed or self-employed person.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>
</tr>

</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>

</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</tr>

</table>

<a id="rct_23"></a>

</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">In view of the differences between the various national systems, it is appropriate that Member States make provision, where possible, for medical treatment for family members of frontier workers in the Member State where the latter pursue their activity.</p>

</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</tr>
</tbody>

</tr>

</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">In order to avoid unwarranted overlapping of benefits, there is a need to lay down rules of priority in the case of overlapping of rights to family benefits under the legislation of the competent Member State and under the legislation of the Member State of residence of the members of the family.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

<a id="rct_36"></a>

<p class="oj-normal">(36)</p>
</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">Advances of maintenance allowances are recoverable advances intended to compensate for a parent's failure to fulfil his legal obligation of maintenance to his own child, which is an obligation derived from family law. Therefore, these advances should not be considered as a direct benefit from collective support in favour of families. Given these particularities, the coordinating rules should not be applied to such maintenance allowances.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>
<td valign="top">
<p class="oj-normal">As the Court of Justice has repeatedly stated, provisions which derogate from the principle of the exportability of social security benefits must be interpreted strictly. This means that they can apply only to benefits which satisfy the specified conditions. It follows that Chapter 9 of Title III of this Regulation can apply only to benefits which are both special and non-contributory and listed in Annex X to this Regulation.</p>
</td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>

</td>
</tr>

<a id="d1e747-1-1"></a>
                     Article 2

<a id="art_2.tit_1"></a>

Persons covered

<a id="002.001"></a>

1.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;This Regulation shall apply to nationals of a Member State, stateless persons and refugees residing in a Member State who are or have been subject to the legislation of one or more Member States, as well as to the members of their families and to their survivors.

<a id="002.002"></a>

2.&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;It shall also apply to the survivors of persons who have been subject to the legislation of one or more Member States, irrespective of the nationality of such persons, where their survivors are nationals of a Member State or stateless persons or refugees residing in one of the Member States.

<a id="art_3"></a>
<!-- /EU legal body -->
